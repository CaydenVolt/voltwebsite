Remarketing is the paid-ads work most solar operators do poorly if they do it at all. The homeowner who visited the quote page, closed the tab and planned to come back later needs a reminder; the lead who filled a form but has not booked a survey needs a different nudge; the visitor who read the EV charger page for four minutes is a different warm lead again. All three can be reached with a remarketing budget that is a small share of total ad spend.

Where it goes wrong is operators treat remarketing as a single display campaign that follows every past visitor with the same ad for 180 days. The ads become background noise, the visitor develops ad blindness, and the operator concludes remarketing does not work. The real failure is undifferentiated audiences, undifferentiated creative, and no exit logic.

This piece lays out the three audiences every solar remarketing account should run, which exclusions matter, which display placements are worth the budget, and what to measure.

## Why does remarketing matter for solar specifically?

Solar is a long-consideration purchase. The typical US residential sale takes 2 to 6 weeks from first interest to signed contract, and the homeowner usually visits three to five vendor websites during that window. A homeowner who hit your quote page on day one and did not fill the form has not disappeared. They are still comparing, still thinking, and the next 30 to 45 days are when remarketing can show up as the quiet reminder that pulls them back to your page.

The specific numbers from audited solar accounts: of visitors who hit a quote or product page and did not submit a form on the first visit, 15 to 25 percent will return within 90 days if any remarketing is running at all. Of those returners, roughly a third convert on the return visit. That is a 5 to 8 percent conversion lift on cold-visitor traffic, available for the cost of a modest display budget.

The other reason: the brand defence benefit. A homeowner who saw your ad once on search, then saw your display ad while reading an unrelated article the next day, then saw your YouTube pre-roll on a cooking video a week later, is a homeowner who is now more likely to type your company name into Google when they are ready. The brand search ad then closes at a $2 to $5 cost per click. Remarketing is partly a loss-leader for brand search conversion.

## What are the three audiences every solar remarketing account needs?

In rough order of value:

**Quote-form visitors who did not submit.** The hottest cold audience. The visitor was on the form, which means they came close to a decision. The exit was for a reason: distracted, interrupted, researching further, waiting to talk to a spouse. The remarketing ad's job is to be visible when the window reopens. Use the website visitor pixel with the condition "visited quote page" AND "did not visit thank-you page". Audience size for a solar account doing 1,000 to 3,000 monthly site visitors: usually 100 to 400 people on a 90-day window.

**Submitted leads who have not booked a survey.** The booked-not-sat audience. These are people who filled your form and had their first conversation with your rep but have not yet scheduled a formal site survey. In solar, this group is 30 to 50 percent of form fills, and some of them will book if nudged. Build this audience from a CRM-fed customer match list; refresh it weekly. Audience size for a mid-sized account: usually 50 to 200 at any given time.

**Product-page viewers who stayed longer than two minutes.** The research audience. People reading your residential, battery, or EV charger product page for two or more minutes have intent but have not acted on it. They may not have been ready to submit the form on that visit. Build with "visited product page" AND "time on page > 120 seconds". Audience size: usually 150 to 500 on 90-day window.

These three audiences cover roughly 80 percent of the remarketing opportunity in a solar account. Additional audiences (visited the pricing page but did not get to the quote form, abandoned a solar calculator, downloaded a guide) can layer on if volume supports it.

## What exclusions matter?

Three exclusions prevent the remarketing budget from leaking:

**Existing customers.** Upload a customer match list and set it as an exclusion. There is no point in paying for ad impressions to the person who signed last month. For solar, this list should include anyone with an active installed system (lifetime) and anyone in-flight (quoted, signed, awaiting install). Refresh monthly.

**Past 90 days without a visit.** A visitor who last touched your site 150 days ago is cold for solar purposes. The purchase window has closed; they bought elsewhere or decided not to buy at all. Set your audience windows at 90 days maximum. Google will offer 540 days as an option; it is rarely useful for solar.

**Converted leads who have booked a survey.** Once they are in the pipeline, further remarketing is wasted budget and sometimes an irritant. Build a dynamic exclusion using the CRM's "survey booked" status, hashed and uploaded to Google as a customer match exclusion.

## Which display placements and campaign types work?

Three recommendations here, from most to least important:

**Run display primarily through PMax's remarketing surface, not a standalone display campaign.** This is a shift from the older advice. Google has steadily deprecated standalone display remarketing in favour of surfacing it inside PMax. For an account running PMax (see the [PMax piece](/blog/solar-google-ads-performance-max-for-installers)), feed your remarketing audiences into PMax as audience signals and let the algorithm handle placement. For an account not yet on PMax, a classic display remarketing campaign still works but will likely be the next piece to migrate.

**Run YouTube remarketing as a parallel channel.** YouTube remarketing (served to past visitors on YouTube in-stream and bumper placements) often shows up as the second-strongest remarketing surface for solar. 15 to 30 second video creative on YouTube reinforces the brand recognition work your other channels are doing. Budget: $20 to $50 per day on a small account; higher if the videos are strong.

**Avoid the display network's generic placements.** Mobile app ad placements rarely convert for solar, and much of the raw display inventory on random content sites is low-quality. Exclude mobile app traffic specifically; apply the standard "sensitive content" filter plus the "social casino" and "non-family-safe" exclusions.

A sprawling display campaign serving across hundreds of placements almost always wastes budget. Simpler, with 3 to 10 placements that actually convert, consistently beats "set and forget" on this channel.

## What creative does remarketing need?

The creative mistake most solar accounts make is reusing the search ad copy as the display ad copy. The two channels have different jobs. Search ads answer a direct question; remarketing ads remind a visitor who already asked that question that you are still here.

Creative that works for solar remarketing:

**Image ads: specific proof, not product shots.** A photo of an installer on a roof in a named town, with a one-line proof point ("40+ installs in Pflugerville in 2026"). An image ad showing a happy customer with first name and town. These outperform generic solar panel photos by 50 to 100 percent in audited accounts.

**Video ads: 15 to 30 seconds, one message, one action.** The video should answer one specific question (how long does install take, what does the first visit look like, what the finance options are) and end with a call to action to book or learn more. Avoid long, polished brand videos; they look like a different company's budget and attract clicks that do not convert.

**Headline language: continuation, not acquisition.** "Still thinking about solar?" works. "Save money on your energy bill" does not; the visitor already knows that, which is why they visited the first time. Address the fact that they are a returning visitor; the ad acknowledging the relationship outperforms the generic one.

**Rotate at least four creative variants.** The same ad for 90 days develops ad blindness; the viewer stops seeing it. Rotate four or more creative options and refresh them quarterly.

## What should the budget be?

Modest. For most solar accounts, remarketing (across display and YouTube) should run at 10 to 15 percent of total paid-ads budget, no more. Beyond that, the remarketing budget is competing for impressions against a limited audience and the frequency per visitor climbs into the "stalker" range, which hurts brand perception without improving conversion.

For a solar account spending $5,000 per month on search plus LSA, that is $500 to $750 per month on remarketing. Enough to run display and YouTube remarketing against two or three audiences with meaningful frequency, not so much that the audience is saturated.

Frequency cap targets:

- Display: 5 to 8 impressions per visitor per month
- YouTube: 3 to 5 impressions per visitor per month

Above these, the ad becomes background noise and clickthrough rate drops.

## How do you measure remarketing success?

Different metrics than search. Remarketing finishes deals the first click started, so attributing it fairly requires looking at assisted conversions, not just last-click.

**Primary metric: assisted conversion rate.** How often does a visitor touched by a remarketing impression ultimately convert, versus a comparable visitor not touched by remarketing? GA4 or your attribution tool should let you cut this. If the lift is in the range of 20 to 40 percent, remarketing is doing its job.

**Secondary metric: brand search volume.** When remarketing is running, brand search impressions and clicks typically rise by 10 to 25 percent within 60 to 90 days. The visitor saw the display ad, remembered the brand, and typed it later. Track brand search growth as a lagging indicator.

**Tertiary metric: cost per conversion on last-click.** This will look worse than search, as expected. If last-click cost per conversion is more than 3 times the search cost per conversion, the audience targeting or creative is probably off; investigate.

Judging remarketing by last-click cost per conversion alone will almost always understate its value. The assisted-conversion lens is where the real work shows up.

## What are the common mistakes?

**One audience, 180-day window, same ad.** The pattern we see most. The audience is too big, too stale, and too undifferentiated to serve usefully, and the one ad burns out after 30 days. Fix: three audiences, 90-day windows, four creative variants rotating.

**Not excluding existing customers.** Running remarketing against people who already bought is paying twice for the same relationship. Add the customer match exclusion.

**Judging by last-click metrics.** Remarketing's job is to assist search and brand clicks that close the deal. Last-click will always look expensive. Judge by assisted conversion lift.

**Over-saturating with frequency.** Six to eight display impressions a day on the same visitor is not reinforcement; it is harassment. Set a frequency cap.

**Running remarketing without conversion tracking in place.** Same rule as every other paid channel. Without the sat appointment flowing back as an offline conversion, you cannot judge any of this. See the [conversion tracking piece](/blog/solar-google-ads-conversion-tracking).

## FAQ

**Can I run remarketing without a display campaign budget?**
Yes, via YouTube remarketing alone. YouTube bumper and short in-stream placements at $10 to $25 per day can carry a solar brand's remarketing on their own if display budget is tight.

**How quickly does a remarketing audience refresh?**
Website visitor audiences update near-real-time (minutes to hours). Customer match uploads refresh at your upload cadence. Weekly uploads are a good baseline for most solar accounts; daily if volume is high.

**Can I remarket to visitors who did not fill a form but called a tracked number?**
Yes, if the call tracking integration pushes the "called" event into GA4 or the pixel. Treat those visitors as a conversion for audience purposes rather than putting them back in the cart-abandoner audience.

**Does remarketing work better on mobile or desktop?**
For solar, impressions skew mobile (60 to 75 percent of inventory) but conversions are more mixed. Display remarketing on mobile converts well for brand recall; the actual form fill often happens on desktop later. Measure assisted conversions across devices rather than last-click per device.

**How long until remarketing starts producing lift?**
Measurable brand search lift usually appears 60 to 90 days after remarketing starts, as the audience builds and the frequency reaches useful levels. Assisted conversion lift shows earlier, usually inside 30 days, if the audiences are built correctly.
