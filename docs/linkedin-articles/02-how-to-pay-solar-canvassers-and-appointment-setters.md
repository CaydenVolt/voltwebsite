```
How to pay solar canvassers and appointment setters
```

## Body

Pay structure is the single biggest reason a solar canvassing team either hums or quietly implodes. Owners get it wrong in two directions: paying so little that nobody good stays, or paying on so many triggers that the setters game the funnel and the install crew eats the bill.

The structure that works for most US residential solar companies is a modest base plus per-sat-appointment plus per-closed-deal, with the per-sat portion weighted toward quality and the per-close portion weighted toward volume. Typical ranges: canvassers earn $50 to $200 per confirmed appointment and $200 to $800 per closed sale; appointment setters earn $25 to $75 per sat appointment and $100 to $400 per closed sale. The exact numbers depend on your geography, your average system size and your close rate. The exact structure depends on how you want behaviour to bend.

This piece covers what to pay, which triggers to pay on, where the structure most commonly breaks, and what we see working across Volt clients today.

## What are you actually paying canvassers and setters for?

Four possible outputs, in order of how directly they tie to revenue:

**A knock or a dial.** The input. Nobody pays for this on its own because activity without result is cost without value. But owners who stop measuring it discover their canvassers were doing 40 knocks a day, not 120, two months after the pipeline has already dried up.

**A booked appointment.** The first result. Easy to measure, easy to game. A booked appointment is one entry on the calendar. It is not yet a conversation.

**A sat appointment.** The appointment the homeowner actually attends and lets your rep run through the full pitch. The industry standard split between booked and sat is 50 to 70 percent, depending on whether appointments are self-booked online or set by a canvasser or setter.

**A closed sale.** The outcome you care about. Also the outcome the canvasser or setter has the least direct control over, since it depends on the closing rep. Paying only on close penalises setters for sending high-quality leads to a weak closer.

Any pay structure is a weighting of these four. Pay too much on the first two and quality collapses. Pay too much on the last and setters stop sending leads to reps they do not trust.

## What are the common pay structures and when does each work?

**Pure per-set.** Setter paid a flat amount per sat appointment, nothing else. Simple to run, easy to understand. Typical: $75 to $150 per sat for appointment setters, $100 to $200 per sat for door canvassers. Works when: your close rate is highly consistent and you want setters to focus purely on volume. Breaks when: setters start sending low-intent appointments that technically "sit" but never close.

**Per-set plus per-close.** Setter earns a smaller per-sat and a larger per-close bonus. Typical: $50 per sat plus $300 to $500 per closed deal. Works when: you want quality bias without taking away the income floor. Most common structure for mature solar teams. Breaks when: the close rate is unstable because of install capacity or finance issues outside the setter's control.

**Per-close only, high rate.** Setter earns nothing per sat, $500 to $1,000 per closed deal. Works when: you have a strong closer, deep pipeline backup for the setter's living expenses, and setters who tolerate variance. Breaks when: setters cannot survive the three to six weeks between sitting and closing. Churn rate climbs.

**Tiered per-close.** Setter earns a base per-close rate and jumps to a higher rate after hitting a monthly threshold. Example: $300 per close for the first eight closes, $500 per close for every close above eight. Works when: you want to drive top-performer stretch effort. Breaks when: setters game by holding deals back to next month to clear the threshold.

**Hourly plus bonus.** Base hourly rate, plus a bonus for sat appointments and/or closes. Common for in-office appointment setters working batched outbound dial sessions. Hourly tends to be $18 to $28 depending on state minimum wage and local labour market, with $25 to $75 per sat. Works when: your state's wage law or classification rules push you toward W2 hourly. Breaks when: the base swallows the bonus pool and the incentive signal gets lost.

## How much should you actually pay per set, per sit and per close?

The ranges we see on well-run US residential teams, net of base pay:

**Canvassers (door-to-door):**
- $50 to $200 per sat appointment
- $200 to $800 per closed sale
- Low end: smaller systems ($15k average), tighter close rates
- High end: large systems ($30k+ average), premium markets like Southern California or South Florida

**Appointment setters (phone or online):**
- $25 to $75 per sat appointment
- $100 to $400 per closed sale
- Lower than canvassers because the setter is working warm inbound leads or re-engaging aged leads, not generating awareness from scratch

**Dedicated outbound SDRs calling cold:**
- $18 to $28 per hour base plus $40 to $100 per sat
- The hourly is higher because cold outbound is unpleasant and demanding; nobody does it well for commission only

The total expected monthly earnings for a performer in each role, assuming average residential volume:

- Canvasser clearing 20 sat appointments and 5 closes a month: $2,500 to $8,000
- Setter clearing 40 sat appointments and 10 closes a month: $2,000 to $7,000
- SDR clearing 150 dials-per-day across 20 working days, averaging 12 sat appointments: $4,500 to $6,500

If your pay structure produces numbers below the low end, your best performers will leave within a quarter. If it produces numbers above the high end, you are overpaying relative to the margin on the closed deal.

## How do canvassers and appointment setters differ in what you should pay?

Canvassers do harder work, from a sales psychology standpoint. They stand on strangers' doorsteps, get rejected 90 percent of the time, and work exposed to weather. They need more income protection and more immediate reward, which means a higher per-sat and faster payment cadence. If your canvassers wait more than two weeks for the sat payout, your best ones leave.

Appointment setters typically work warm or re-engagement lists. The emotional labour is lower, the volume is higher, and the per-sat payout can be smaller. But they tend to be more sensitive to lead quality: an SDR who is handed a bad list for two weeks straight will quit, even at a good base.

The honest division of pay should match the division of effort. A canvasser creating awareness from scratch earns two to three times what a setter re-engaging a warm lead earns, per sat appointment, because the two outputs are not the same output.

## What are the structure mistakes that burn cash?

**Paying only on booked, not on sat.** Setters book phantom appointments. Install crews chase ghosts. Pay only on sat.

**Paying the same for a cash sale and a financed sale.** A cash install is roughly 20 to 30 percent more margin for you than a financed install because of dealer fees. If your pay structure treats them the same, you have no incentive to coach reps toward cash. Pay a bigger bonus on cash sales.

**Clawback timed wrong.** A deal that cancels inside the three-day right of rescission is universally clawed back. A deal that cancels at permit stage three months later is not. Where is your clawback window? If it extends past PTO, you are not paying your setter their full earnings until the funder has disbursed, which can be 90 days or more. Setters notice.

**Per-close bonus so large that reps stop canvassing.** When the per-close is 10 times the per-sat, canvassers follow deals from first knock to closed install and stop generating new appointments. Pipeline dries up two months later. Weight so that steady canvassing out-earns one-off closing.

**No kill switch for stacked incentives.** A canvasser who hits a per-sat bonus AND a weekly target bonus AND a monthly top-performer bonus can earn more than the closed deal's margin. Add a cap or a tiered schedule that keeps total comp inside a sane percentage of gross profit (we see teams target 15 to 25 percent of gross profit on the deal).

## How do you prevent people from gaming the system?

Three mechanisms, in order of how blunt they are:

**Pay on the right trigger.** Pay on sat, not on booked. Pay on PTO or close, not on signed contract. Each step adds friction for the gaming behaviour and reduces payouts to people who were producing phantom work.

**Sample-check the sats.** Pull a random 10 to 15 percent of recorded sat appointments each week and have the sales manager listen or watch them. If more than one in ten was obviously a phoner-pitch to meet quota, the setter gets a warning, then a performance plan, then a termination. Everyone knows there is spot-checking.

**Tier the per-close by close rate.** Setters whose sat-to-close rate stays above a threshold (say 25 percent) earn the full per-close bonus. Setters below that threshold earn 60 to 70 percent of it. Automatic self-correction: a setter sending bad leads earns less without a manager having a conversation.

## What this looks like in practice for a mid-sized residential installer

A company doing 25 installs a month with three canvassers and two in-office setters. Canvassers earn $100 per sat plus $400 per close, averaging 8 closes a month each (24 total across the team). Setters earn $40 per sat plus $200 per close, averaging 4 closes each (8 total). Base pay is zero for canvassers (1099), $20 per hour for setters (W2).

Monthly comp pool: canvassers $400 per close × 24 = $9,600; plus $100 per sat × roughly 70 sats per canvasser = $21,000. Total canvasser comp: $30,600 across three people, or $10,200 each (within the healthy performer range).

Setter comp: $200 per close × 8 = $1,600; $40 per sat × roughly 60 sats per setter = $4,800; plus base of roughly $3,500 per setter per month. Total setter comp: $9,900 across two people, or $4,950 each.

Total team comp: $40,500 across 25 closed installs at an average $23,000 revenue per deal. Pay-to-revenue ratio: 7 percent, which sits in the sweet spot for canvassing + setting operations (6 to 9 percent).

## FAQ

**Should canvassers be 1099 or W2?**
Depends on state. In California and a growing number of states, the ABC test for independent contractor classification makes 1099 canvassers risky. In those states, W2 is the safe default even if it costs more in payroll taxes and workers comp. In states with looser classification rules, 1099 is still viable but requires actual independence (canvasser sets own hours, uses own tools, works for multiple companies). Talk to a solar-savvy employment lawyer before deciding; the penalty for misclassification can run tens of thousands per worker.

**How often should I pay?**
Weekly on sat appointment bonuses and biweekly on closed-deal bonuses. The faster the payment cycle, the lower the churn, especially for canvassers. A commission check that lands three weeks after the work was done is almost as demotivating as not paying at all.

**What is a reasonable close rate from a sat appointment?**
25 to 40 percent for warm leads with a qualified sat, 15 to 25 percent for cold door-to-door sats. If your rate is below those ranges, the problem is almost always the closer, not the setter. Fix the closing before you blame the setters.

**Should the sales rep's commission be lower if a setter also gets paid on the close?**
Mathematically, yes, if your gross margin is fixed. Practically, if you cut the closing rep's commission to pay the setter, the closer either stops closing or leaves. Better path: find the setter comp inside the deal's acquisition cost budget, not inside the closer's commission. The industry split that holds up: closer gets 4 to 7 percent of system revenue, setter gets the per-sat and per-close bonus, both come out of the lead-acquisition line, not the closing line.

**How do I handle a setter whose appointments sit at the right rate but never close?**
Shadow them for a week. Nine times out of ten the setter is pitching features and pricing too early, which makes the sat appointment "warm" on paper but blown by the time the rep arrives. The fix is scripting discipline, not a comp change.
